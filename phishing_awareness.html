CodeAlpha · Cyber Security Task 2

Pause before you click

A short phishing-awareness lesson. Learn how attackers create urgency and borrow trust, practise checking a message, and test what you would do next.

01 · Understand the tactic

What phishing tries to do

Phishing is a social-engineering attempt to make someone reveal information, send money, open a harmful file, or sign in through a deceptive link. The message may arrive by email, text, phone, social media, or a collaboration app.

Urgency

“Act now or your account will close.” Pressure makes people skip normal checks.

Impersonation

A display name, logo, or familiar writing style can be copied. Check the actual address and confirm unusual requests through a separate channel.

Curiosity or reward

An unexpected invoice, delivery notice, shared file, prize, or refund can tempt a quick click.

02 · Inspect an example

Example: a fake account alert

From: “Campus IT Helpdesk” <security-alert@campus-help.example.net>

Subject: Action required in 30 minutes

Your mailbox will be disabled. Sign in now to keep access: campus-help.example.net/verify

  • The sender’s domain is not the institution’s known domain.
  • A short deadline pushes you to act without checking.
  • The link asks you to sign in from a message you did not expect.

Safer response: do not use the link. Open the official service using a saved bookmark or type its known address, then contact IT through a known channel. The example domain above is reserved for training and is not a real login page.

03 · Use a quick check

Before you respond

Check the request

Was this expected? Is it asking for credentials, payment, personal data, or a change to bank details?

Check independently

Use a known website or phone number. For money or account changes, verify with a second person or established approval process.

Report, then remove

Use your organisation’s report-phishing button or tell its IT/security team. Follow local incident instructions.

If you already clicked or shared information, report it quickly. Change an exposed password from the real site, revoke suspicious sessions if available, and contact your organisation or bank when relevant. Fast reporting can limit further harm.

04 · Check your understanding

Three-question quiz

1. An email says your account closes in 10 minutes and includes a sign-in link. What is the safest next step?
2. A familiar supplier emails new bank details. What should you do?
3. You entered your password on a page from a suspicious link. What now?
